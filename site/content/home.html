TrestleBoard is a free program for one job: producing the trestle board your lodge sends out every month, and the PDF you email round when it is done. It runs on your own computer, keeps your members' details on it and nowhere else, and asks nothing of you but the evening.

Why it is not a word processor

Because a trestle board is a page, not a document. The officers table has to sit in the corner it always sits in, the photograph has to have the article close up behind it, and the whole thing has to come out at four to six sides every time.

TrestleBoard draws its own pages, and the same code draws the screen and the PDF. What you arrange is what prints — not an approximation of it, and not something that reflows on the machine you send it to.

Stated Communication

Brethren, the summer months bring their own rhythm to the lodge. Attendance thins as families travel, and yet the work continues much as it always has. There is a lesson in that steadiness. A lodge is not built out of its busiest nights. It is built out of the ordinary ones, when a handful of brothers turn up because they said they would, and the work gets done because it is there to be done.

That excerpt is set the way the program sets it, in one of the twenty typefaces that ship inside it. None of the fonts installed on your computer is ever used, which is what makes a newsletter fall on the same lines on every machine that opens it.

What a month looks like

Eleven months out of twelve it begins with Start from last month. The officers, the committees and the district table carry across, the dates move on, and last month's articles are cleared away so this month's can be written.
